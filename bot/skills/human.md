# Human summary and cover letter

Load this skill for every application pack, before `save_application`. The professional
summary and the cover letter both go through this pass. Facts stay inside the master CV.

## Summary

- Two sentences, exactly 350 characters, so the one-page Canva frame still fits.
- No "I". It is a CV summary, written the way a person describes work they actually did.
- Keep it general. Do not put percentages, feature names, or a specific result in the summary. Those stay in the experience bullets.
- Sentence one: software engineering background, then the skills that match this posting, such as customer research, product analytics, backlog prioritization, requirements, and cross-functional delivery.
- Sentence two: analytical and technical thinking, a customer-focused approach, and measurable product improvement. No case study.

## Cover letter

Keep it as short as the note below. Do not add a fourth body paragraph.

```
Dear Hiring Team,

I'm excited to apply for the [role]. My background combines hands-on product management and software engineering, with experience in [the skills on the CV that match this posting].

I've used customer insights and product data to identify opportunities and deliver features that increased subscriptions by 12% and conversions by 7%. I'm particularly interested in this role because [one reason from the posting].

I'm based in Armenia and open to relocating to [city] for an on-site role. I'd be glad to discuss how I could contribute to your product team.

Best regards,
Samvel Demirtshyan
```

- Greeting is exactly `Dear Hiring Team,`. Sign-off is `Best regards,` then `Samvel Demirtshyan` on the next line.
- Paragraph one names the role and the matching skills. No metrics there.
- Paragraph two is the only place for the 12% subscriptions and 7% conversions, then why this role.
- Paragraph three says he is based in Armenia. For an on-site role outside Armenia, say he is open to relocating to that city. Then one short close.
- No bullets, headers, or subject line.

## Voice

Write like a careful email, not a brochure. Mix a short sentence with a longer one.

Do not use: delve, leverage, robust, seamless, cutting-edge, passionate, thrilled,
honed, spearheaded, landscape, tapestry, pivotal, foster, utilize, "not only ... but",
"I am writing to express", or a stack of em dashes.

After the draft, read it once and cut anything that sounds generated. Do not add employers,
dates, metrics, tools, or outcomes that are not in the master CV.
